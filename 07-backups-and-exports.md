# Backups and exports

You can only move what you have a copy of. This section is the concrete backup discipline that turns a deplatforming from a catastrophe into an inconvenience. The rule is that no platform ever holds the only copy of anything you need.

## Export your audience contacts regularly

Your owned list is only owned if you have it outside the service that hosts it. Export your contact list on a schedule, not just when something goes wrong, and store the export somewhere you control. If your list provider itself ever restricts you, a recent export means your audience relationship survives the provider. Treat the most recent export as one of your most valuable files.

## Keep masters of all content

Store the original, full-quality files for everything you publish: source video, images, audio, project files, and the text of long posts. Organize them so the catalog can be republished without reconstruction. The platform copy is a convenience; the master is the asset. If you create only inside a platform's tools and never keep the source, you do not actually hold your own work.

## Back up the metadata too

The content is not the only thing worth saving. Keep your titles, descriptions, tags, publish dates, and the analytics that tell you which pieces performed. This metadata lets you rebuild intelligently rather than guessing, and it is part of proving your history if you ever need to.

## Follow a simple redundancy rule

Keep more than one copy, in more than one place, with at least one copy not controlled by the same provider as the others. A single backup on the same service you are backing up is not redundancy. The point is that no single failure, including the failure of a backup provider, leaves you with nothing.

## Test that your backups actually work

A backup you have never restored from is a guess. Periodically confirm that your contact export opens and is complete, that your masters play and are not corrupted, and that you could actually republish from them. The time to discover a broken backup is during a test, not during a recovery.

## Automate what you can, calendar the rest

Manual backups get skipped. Automate the exports and copies that can be automated, and put the ones that cannot on a recurring calendar reminder so they happen on a schedule. Consistency is what makes a backup useful, because the only backup that helps is a recent one.
