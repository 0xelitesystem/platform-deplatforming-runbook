# Owned vs rented

The architecture that survives a deplatforming is built on one distinction: what you own versus what you rent. Owned assets you can export and move. Rented assets can be reduced or removed by someone else. A durable operation keeps the assets it cannot afford to lose on the owned side of that line.

## The two categories

Rented assets include your accounts and followings on any platform, your standing with a recommendation algorithm, your ad accounts, and anything where access depends on a third party's ongoing permission. Owned assets include your email and messaging lists where you hold the contacts, your content masters in storage you control, your own website or domain, and your direct relationships with customers and audience. The test is simple: if the company disappeared tomorrow, would you still have it.

## The hub-and-spoke model

A resilient structure puts an owned asset at the center as the hub, usually the email list and a site on a domain you control, and treats every platform as a spoke. Each spoke does two jobs: it reaches new people, and it routes them to the hub. The spokes are expendable by design. Losing one hurts, but the hub and the audience it holds remain. Compare this to the fragile model where the platform is the center and everything depends on it staying.

## Diversify spokes across failure modes

Not all spokes fail the same way. A long-form video platform, a short-form feed, a text platform, and a marketplace each have different policies and different triggers. Spreading presence across platforms that do not share a failure mode means a single policy change or suspension is unlikely to take all of them at once. Two accounts on the same platform are one failure, not two.

## Hold the keys to your own distribution

Owning a domain you control matters more than it appears. Email sent from your own domain, a site you host, and links that point to your domain rather than a platform profile all give you distribution that no platform can switch off. When a spoke is lost, your domain-based presence is the address that still works and the one you can point everyone back to.

## Invest in the hub continuously

The hub only protects you if it is large and current when you need it. That means steady investment in list growth and direct relationships during the good times, when there is no fire and the work feels optional. The operators who are fine after a loss are the ones who treated hub growth as the main job, not an afterthought to platform reach.
