# The first hours

When an account is restricted, suspended, or removed, the first hours set up everything that follows. Move deliberately. The goal is to preserve evidence, reach your audience through channels that still work, and avoid actions that hurt an appeal.

## Stop and capture evidence first

Before you click anything, document the current state. Screenshot the notice, the account status, any stated reason, and the date and time. Save any email you received. If you can still see your content or analytics, capture them. This record is the basis of any appeal and your proof of what happened and when.

## Do not rage-react

Do not delete content, do not fire off angry messages through official channels, and do not create a pile of new accounts in the first hour. Impulsive actions can be read as evasion and can weaken a legitimate appeal. The instinct to do something immediately is strong and usually counterproductive. The productive immediate action is preservation, not reaction.

## Reach your audience through a channel that still works

This is the moment your owned list earns its existence. Send a calm, factual message to your email or messaging list: what happened, where to find you now, and what to expect next. Keep it free of blame and drama. If you have a secondary platform presence, post the same redirect there. The aim is continuity, so your audience does not simply lose track of you.

## Identify exactly what was lost

Be precise about the scope. Is it a single piece of content, a feature restriction, a temporary suspension, or full removal? Is it one account or several tied together? Does it affect payments or only reach? The response to a single removed post is not the response to a full account loss, and confusing them wastes the hours that matter.

## Check linked exposure

If the affected account is linked to others, to payment processing, or to logins you use elsewhere, check those immediately. A suspension on one service sometimes cascades to linked services. Knowing the full blast radius in the first hours prevents a second surprise later.

## Start the appeal clock

Find the official appeal path and note any deadline. Many appeal windows are short. You do not have to submit a perfect appeal in the first hour, but you do need to know the deadline before it passes. Then move to the appeals process deliberately rather than in a panic.
