# Warning signs

A reach throttle or shadowban rarely comes with a notice. It shows up as numbers, and your job is to separate a real suppression from ordinary variance. Overreacting to a slow week is as costly as ignoring a genuine throttle.

## What a throttle looks like in the data

The pattern that should get your attention is a sharp, sustained drop in reach that is not explained by your own posting changes. Signs include impressions falling well below your established baseline across multiple posts, a collapse in the share of views coming from discovery or recommendation surfaces while your direct and follower traffic holds, and new content failing to reach even a fraction of your existing audience.

## Distinguishing it from normal variance

Before concluding anything, rule out the ordinary causes. Did you change posting time, format, topic, or frequency? Is it a known slow period? Is the drop on one platform or all of them at once, which would point to something on your side? Look at a rolling baseline over weeks, not a single bad day. A real throttle is broad, sustained, and concentrated in algorithmic distribution while your owned channels are unaffected.

## Check the reachable-audience signal

One of the clearest tests is whether your own followers are seeing you. If people who explicitly chose to follow you stop receiving your posts, that is different from discovery cooling off. Ask your owned list directly whether they are still seeing your platform content. Because you can reach the list without the platform, it gives you a clean read the platform's own numbers cannot.

## Keep a baseline so you can tell

None of this works without a baseline. Record your normal reach, discovery share, and follower-view rate while things are healthy. Suppression is only visible against a known normal. The operators who detect throttles early are the ones who were already watching the right numbers before anything went wrong.

## Do not spiral

A confirmed throttle is a problem to manage, not a catastrophe to panic over. Panic posting, deleting content, or rapid format changes in response to a suspected throttle often make the read harder and the situation worse. Confirm calmly, then act on the plan you prepared.
