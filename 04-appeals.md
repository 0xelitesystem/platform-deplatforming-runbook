# Appeals

An appeal is a request to a process that is mostly automated, often under-resourced, and not built around your case. Understanding that shapes how you write one. A good appeal is calm, specific, short, and makes the reviewer's decision easy. A bad appeal is long, emotional, and argues with the platform.

## Understand what you are appealing to

Most first-line review is automated or handled at high volume with little time per case. The reviewer is not your adversary and usually has no context. Your appeal succeeds by being easy to approve, not by being persuasive in the way a human argument would be. Write for a tired reviewer with thirty seconds, not for a judge.

## What tends to help

State plainly that you believe an error was made, identify the account and the specific action, and give the concrete reason the decision does not fit your case. If a policy was cited, address that exact policy and explain specifically why your content does not violate it. Reference your history of good standing if you have it. Be factual and brief. Make it trivial to verify that you are a legitimate account that was caught wrongly.

## What tends to hurt

Long emotional letters, threats, public pressure campaigns aimed at the reviewer, accusations of bias, and walls of text all reduce your odds. So does submitting many appeals in parallel or reopening the same case repeatedly, which can flag the account further. Resist the urge to vent through the official channel. Vent to your list or a friend, not to the form.

## Submit once, well, then wait

Put your effort into one clear, complete appeal rather than ten hasty ones. After submitting, give the process its stated time before following up. Repeated submissions rarely speed anything and sometimes signal evasion. Use the waiting time to advance your rebuilding plan so you are not idle and not dependent on the appeal succeeding.

## Plan for the appeal to fail

Many appeals are denied or never meaningfully answered. Treat a successful appeal as a bonus, not a plan. The moment you submit the appeal, your real work shifts to rebuilding on ground you control, because that is the outcome you can actually influence.
